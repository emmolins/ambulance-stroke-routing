\section{Problem Formulation}

\jama{JAMA "MDP Framework" opening, kept.}
We model prehospital stroke triage as a Markov decision process (MDP)~\cite{bellman1957,kochenderfer2022} defined by a state space, an action space, a transition model, and a reward function. The objective is a policy, a mapping from states to actions, that maximises expected cumulative reward. Here the horizon is short: an initial transport and at most one inter-facility transfer.

\subsection{Hospital capability tiers}
\new{Hospitals are classified by what they can do for a stroke patient on arrival, not by certification label. An \emph{EVT-capable centre} performs endovascular thrombectomy on site; in the primary analysis this means a Joint Commission Comprehensive or Thrombectomy-Capable Stroke Center, a DNV Comprehensive Stroke Center, or a hospital designated for thrombectomy by its county EMS agency. A \emph{thrombolysis-capable centre} administers intravenous thrombolysis but transfers patients for EVT (Primary, Advanced Primary and Acute Stroke Ready certification). A \emph{non-stroke-centre hospital} has a 24-hour emergency department and no stroke certification; we assume it neither administers thrombolysis nor performs EVT and transfers for both. Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes how the designations were verified, and Section~\ref{sec:results} reports a sensitivity analysis in which hospitals known to perform thrombectomy without certification are treated as EVT-capable.}

\subsection{State space}
\jama{JAMA, edited: onset range and subtype prior kept; "assigned a subtype" clarified (Reviewer 2).}
A state $s = (\ell, t, k, \sigma)$ comprises the patient's location $\ell$ (a pickup coordinate or a hospital), minutes since symptom onset $t$, whether the subtype is known $k \in \{\text{unknown}, \text{known}\}$, and the subtype $\sigma \in \{\text{LVO}, \text{nLVO}, \text{ICH}, \text{mimic}\}$. Pickup locations are sampled from high-resolution population density data~\cite{meta2019}, and onset-to-pickup time from 30 to 270 minutes, reflecting typical ranges to EMS contact~\cite{eddelien2021}. \new{The subtype is drawn from the published prevalence of each category among patients with a positive prehospital LVO screen (LAMS~$\geq$~4)~\cite{holodinsky2018}: $p_{\text{LVO}} = 0.454$, $p_{\text{nLVO}} = 0.109$, $p_{\text{ICH}} = 0.345$, $p_{\text{mimic}} = 0.092$. The planner never observes the drawn subtype while $k = \text{unknown}$; it reasons over the prior (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The subtype becomes known on arrival at any hospital, when imaging is assumed to occur.}

\subsection{Action space}
\jama{JAMA, with tier names replaced.}
Actions are discrete and location-dependent, and never route a patient to a lower capability tier. From the field, the patient may be transported to any hospital within the EMS catchment radius (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). From a non-stroke-centre hospital the options are transfer to a thrombolysis-capable or EVT-capable centre, or stay. From a thrombolysis-capable centre, transfer to an EVT-capable centre or stay. At an EVT-capable centre the only action is to stay.

\subsection{Transition model}
\jama{JAMA, rewritten: the single 60-min "preparation time" is replaced by the interval model below (Reviewers 1 and 2).}
A routing action moves the patient to the chosen hospital and advances the clock by the road travel time $\tau(\ell, \ell')$ obtained from the OpenRouteService routing engine on OpenStreetMap data; a transfer out of a hospital additionally incurs the door-in-door-out interval $T_{\text{DIDO}}$ before departure. On arrival the subtype becomes known. Transitions are deterministic given the action; all uncertainty enters through the subtype.

\subsection{In-hospital intervals}
\new{Treatment is not instantaneous on arrival. We model three intervals, one value per interval for all hospitals of a tier, taken from national benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:intervals}): door-to-needle $T_{\text{DTN}}$ for thrombolysis; door-to-puncture $T_{\text{DTP}}$ for EVT after direct arrival, and a shorter $T_{\text{DTP}}^{\text{tr}}$ for a transferred-in patient who arrives already imaged; and door-in-door-out $T_{\text{DIDO}}$ at the sending hospital. For a patient arriving directly at an EVT-capable centre at onset time $t_a$, needle time is $t_a + T_{\text{DTN}}$ and puncture time $t_a + T_{\text{DTP}}$. At a thrombolysis-capable centre, needle time is $t_a + T_{\text{DTN}}$ and puncture time, via the nearest EVT-capable centre at road time $\tau'$, is $t_a + T_{\text{DIDO}} + \tau' + T_{\text{DTP}}^{\text{tr}}$. At a non-stroke-centre hospital both treatments follow a transfer. Inter-facility transfers are not distance-limited; a pair is unreachable only if the road network has no route.}

\new{For comparison, Holodinsky \emph{et al.}~\cite{holodinsky2018} assume door-to-needle 30, door-in-door-out 50 and door-to-puncture 60 (direct) or 30 (transfer) minutes in their base case, and 60 / 120 / 60--90 minutes in their slower scenarios; our base case lies between the two and is anchored to current registry medians and national targets.}

\subsection{Reward}
\jama{JAMA "Reward Function" + eMethods 1 equations moved into the main text. Define "good outcome" explicitly (Reviewer 2).}
The reward of reaching a terminal hospital state is the probability of a good outcome, defined as an excellent outcome at 90 days (modified Rankin Scale 0--1), following Holodinsky \emph{et al.}~\cite{holodinsky2018}. For ischaemic subtypes it declines with onset-to-treatment time; for haemorrhage and mimics it is constant. With $t_n$ and $t_p$ the onset-to-needle and onset-to-puncture times in minutes,
\begin{align}
P_{\text{LVO,IVT}}(t_n) &= \max\{0.1328,\; 0.2359 - 0.0004\,t_n + 2\times10^{-7}\,t_n^2\},\\
P_{\text{LVO,EVT}}(t_p) &= \max\{0.129,\; 0.3394 - 0.0002\,t_p + 4\times10^{-8}\,t_p^2\},\\
P_{\text{LVO}} &= P_{\text{LVO,IVT}} + (1 - P_{\text{LVO,IVT}})\,P_{\text{LVO,EVT}},\\
P_{\text{nLVO}}(t_n) &= \max\{0.4622,\; 0.6343 - 0.0005\,t_n - 5\times10^{-8}\,t_n^2\},\\
P_{\text{ICH}} &= 0.24, \qquad P_{\text{mimic}} = 0.90,
\end{align}
where EVT is available only if an EVT-capable centre is reachable (otherwise $P_{\text{LVO,EVT}} = 0$) and $t_n, t_p \geq 270$ minutes yield the floor values. \new{While the subtype is unknown the reward is the prior-weighted mixture $\sum_\sigma p_\sigma P_\sigma$; once known, the subtype-specific function applies. The planner therefore optimises expected outcome under subtype uncertainty rather than for a label it does not have (Reviewer 2's question).}
