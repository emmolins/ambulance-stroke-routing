\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Regions and hospital networks}
We evaluate two regions with different network structures and stroke-system policies: the nine-county San Francisco Bay Area, California, a decentralized system with 11 EVT-capable centers, 44 thrombolysis-capable centers and 6 non-stroke-center hospitals (61 in total), and the state of Rhode Island, a centralized system with one EVT-capable center and eight thrombolysis-capable centers, which has been the focus of stroke policy improvements~\cite{jayaraman2023}. Hospital designations were verified in October 2026 against county EMS agency receiving-facility lists, the Rhode Island Department of Health stroke designation list, certifying-body records and hospital system pages; coordinates were checked against street addresses. Four Bay Area hospitals perform thrombectomy without Comprehensive or Thrombectomy-Capable certification; they are thrombolysis-capable in the primary analysis and EVT-capable in a sensitivity analysis. Designations change (two Bay Area hospitals changed tier in 2026), so the hospital table is an input to the method rather than part of it. Routing protocols also differ within the Bay Area: San Francisco routes to the closest stroke center without severity tiering, Santa Clara County routes screen-positive patients to the closest EVT-capable center, and Marin County transfers LVO patients out of county, often by air.

\subsection{Simulated cohorts}
Each replicate samples 1,000 pickup locations without replacement from a population-weighted pool of 10,000 points~\cite{meta2019}, draws the subtype from the LAMS-positive prior, and routes every patient under each policy. Onset-to-pickup time, the interval from symptom onset (last known well) through the call to EMS, response and scene time to departure, is drawn from a log-normal distribution with median 90 minutes and log-scale standard deviation 1.28, truncated to 30--270 minutes. The parameters are the onset-to-door quantiles of EMS-attended, EVT-eligible patients in a regional stroke network (median 104, IQR 60--338 minutes)~\cite{hegenberg2026} less the U.S. median EMS transport interval of 14 minutes~\cite{cash2022,chari2022}; the resulting first-contact times are consistent with LAMS-positive patients in Los Angeles County (median 26 minutes to first medical contact among those treated within 6 hours)~\cite{bosson2023} and the 30-minute floor with national response and scene intervals~\cite{chari2022}. Within the window, 30\% of patients depart within an hour of onset and 16\% after three hours. Alternative parameterizations fitted to all-severity EMS arrivals~\cite{adeoye2017} change these shares by at most three points once truncated to the window, because the heavy tail on which the sources differ lies beyond it. A uniform draw on 30--270 minutes, which over-represents late presenters, is retained as a sensitivity cohort. We run 10 replicates per region with distinct seeds, giving 10,000 patients per policy and between-replicate confidence intervals. The same patients are routed under every policy, so comparisons are paired. A patient is replaced only if the routing engine cannot route from the sampled point under some policy (none of 10,000 in the Bay Area, 1 of 10,000 in Rhode Island). The 200$\times$200 grid used for the geographic analysis (five patients per cell) is the visualization layer, not the cohort behind the main results.

\subsection{Travel times and catchment}
Travel times are computed on the OpenStreetMap road network with OpenRouteService~8.0 (driving-car profile, matrix endpoint) hosted locally. Candidate destinations for the initial transport are restricted to facilities within 80~km of the pickup location, an EMS catchment assumption; inter-facility transfers are computed by road for all pairs without a distance restriction. Travel times are deterministic in the planner; sensitivity to travel-time error is assessed post hoc.

\subsection{Outcome measures and statistics}
The primary measure is the probability of an excellent 90-day outcome (mRS 0--1) per patient. We distinguish the \emph{planning} reward, the prior-weighted expected outcome that the planner optimizes and that a dispatcher faces, from the \emph{realized} reward, the subtype-specific outcome given the drawn subtype. Tables report planning rewards; realized rewards are reported as a check and give the same differences. For each pair of policies we report the mean paired difference with a 95\% confidence interval across the 10 replicates ($t_9$), the paired $t$-test with Bonferroni correction for the five comparisons, and Cohen's $d_z$. Equity is assessed by stratifying paired differences by census-tract median household income quartile and rural-urban commuting area class, for the MDP against nearest-hospital routing and against both heuristics.

\subsection{Sensitivity analyses}
(i) Travel-time error: multiplicative log-normal noise ($\sigma = 0.10, 0.20$) and systematic bias ($\times 0.70$ to $\times 1.30$) applied to the recorded first-leg travel time, with one draw per patient and destination so identical routes receive identical perturbations; rewards are recomputed and the paired comparisons repeated. (ii) In-hospital intervals: $T_{\text{DIDO}} \in \{60, 90, 121, 175\}$ minutes, with rewards recomputed for the policies as planned under the base case. (iii) EVT definition: the four uncertified thrombectomy sites treated as EVT-capable (15 centers), with the policy re-planned. (iv) Onset-time distribution: the uniform cohort, re-simulated with the policy re-planned. (v) Decision-tree depth: 1 to 10 and unconstrained.

\subsection{Latency and reproducibility}
Per-decision wall-clock time of the planner is measured for 50 patients at planning depths 1 to 4 on a laptop (\pending{Apple M-series, xx GB}) with the routing engine on the same machine, with each patient's travel-time row evicted from the cache before timing so the figure includes the routing-engine call. We report mean, median, 95th and 99th percentiles. All randomness is seeded, the Julia environment is pinned, and code and hospital tables will be released on acceptance.
